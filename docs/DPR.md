# Detailed Project Report (DPR)
## PCOS / PCOD & Women's Lifestyle Health App

*All figures are planning estimates built on 2025–2026 market benchmarks. Currency in INR; USD shown at ~₹85/$ for reference. Treat every number as an assumption you can tune, not a guarantee.*

---

## 1. Executive summary

A mobile-first platform that helps women screen for, understand, and manage PCOS/PCOD and broader menstrual and lifestyle health — combining tracking, an evidence-based risk screener, expert teleconsults, lifestyle plans, mental-health support, a partner-education feature, and a peer community, all built privacy-first for the Indian market.

**The market is real and growing:** India's FemTech market was about USD 1.49 billion in 2025 and is projected to grow ~15–16% per year. PCOS affects roughly 8–9% of Indian reproductive-age women (some urban studies report far higher), and the condition is heavily underdiagnosed.

**The honest financial reality:** consumer health apps make almost nothing at small scale. At 1,000 active users you should expect only ~₹40,000–₹70,000 per *year* in gross revenue. Revenue becomes meaningful at 50,000+ engaged users, and early-stage profitability usually depends on **B2B/institutional deals** (colleges, employers, clinics) rather than consumer subscriptions alone. Industry data is sobering: only about 5% of health & fitness apps reach even ~USD 10,000 in total revenue within their first two years. This is a scale-and-retention game.

---

## 2. Project objectives

1. Reduce diagnostic delay by getting at-risk women screened and into clinical care faster.
2. Improve day-to-day PCOS management through lifestyle, mental-health, and tracking tools.
3. Build the most trusted (privacy-first, India-aware, vernacular) women's health app in the market.
4. Reach a sustainable unit economics position by ~100,000 engaged users + institutional partnerships.

## 3. Market analysis

### Market sizing (top-down)

| Layer | Definition | Estimate |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| **TAM** | India FemTech market (all women's health tech) | ~USD 1.49 bn (2025), growing ~15–16%/yr |
| **SAM** | Indian smartphone-using women of reproductive age interested in menstrual/PCOS digital health | Tens of millions of users; PCOS population alone ≈ 25–30 million women |
| **SOM (3-yr)** | Realistic share you can capture and retain | ~100,000–500,000 users |

PCOS prevalence in India is reported between ~4% and 17%+ depending on region and criteria (pooled ~8–9%). Underdiagnosis is severe — one PGIMER study found ~45% of women knew almost nothing about PCOS even after starting treatment. **The awareness gap is your demand engine.**

### Competitive landscape

- **Global generalists:** Flo, Clue, Glow — strong tracking, but poor accuracy for irregular cycles and (for Flo) a damaged privacy reputation (FTC settlement + ~USD 56M class action in 2025).
- **PCOS-specific:** Cysterhood, PCOS Meal Planner, Oana — diet/community focused, mostly Western, USD pricing.
- **India telehealth:** Practo, Apollo 247, etc. — consults but not PCOS-specialized or lifestyle-focused.

**Your gap to own:** a PCOS-specialized, privacy-first, vernacular, India-priced platform that bridges screening → diagnosis → ongoing management. No incumbent does all of that.

## 4. Target users

- **Primary:** Women 18–35 in India with diagnosed or suspected PCOS/PCOD (urban + tier-2/3, English + regional languages).
- **Secondary:** Adolescents and their families; women seeking general menstrual/fertility tracking; partners (education feature).
- **B2B buyers:** Colleges/universities, employers (wellness programs), gynae clinics, diagnostic labs, insurers.

---

## 5. Go-to-market & marketing strategy

**Positioning:** *"The trustworthy PCOS companion that gets you the right answers, the right doctor, and a healthier routine — privately."*

**Wedge (launch focus):** the screener + tracker + auto-generated Doctor Report. Be the best at one thing first.

### Channels (in rough priority/cost order)

| Channel | Why it fits | Cost |
|---------|-------------|------|
| **Content & SEO** (PCOS education in English + Malayalam/Hindi/Tamil) | Huge awareness gap = high-intent search demand; compounds over time | Low (time) |
| **Health creators / influencers** (PCOS "cyster" community, gynae creators on IG/YouTube) | Trust-driven category; creators convert well | Medium |
| **Peer community & word-of-mouth** | Chronic condition → sticky, referral-friendly | Low |
| **College / campus partnerships** | Young women = high PCOS prevalence; doubles as B2B seed | Low–Medium |
| **Clinic / gynae referrals** | Doctors recommend it for tracking + Doctor Report; high-trust acquisition | Low |
| **ASO (App Store Optimization)** | Capture "PCOS app / period tracker" searches | Low |
| **Paid ads (IG/Google)** | Scale once unit economics are proven | High — use sparingly early |

### Phasing

- **Phase 1 (0–6 mo):** Organic content, community seeding, 2–3 creator partnerships, ASO, 1–2 pilot college tie-ups.
- **Phase 2 (6–18 mo):** Scale creators, clinic referral network, launch teleconsults, begin paid acquisition where CAC < LTV.
- **Phase 3 (18 mo+):** B2B sales motion (employers/insurers), regional-language expansion, performance marketing at scale.

## 6. Traffic & user-growth projections (illustrative funnel)

Health-app funnels are leaky — typical day-30 retention is low industry-wide, though a *chronic* condition like PCOS helps retention versus generic fitness apps. Illustrative base case:

| Stage | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|-------|-------:|-------:|-------:|
| App downloads | 50,000 | 200,000 | 500,000 |
| Activation (complete onboarding) ~35% | 17,500 | 70,000 | 175,000 |
| Retained monthly active users (MAU) | 10,000–15,000 | 40,000–60,000 | 100,000–150,000 |
| Paying subscribers (~3% of MAU) | ~300–450 | ~1,200–1,800 | ~3,000–4,500 |

These are stretch-but-plausible if content + community + referrals work. Many apps grow far slower — do not bank on these without traction signals.

## 7. Revenue model

Four streams, deliberately **excluding ad-funded data-selling** (your trust differentiator):

1. **Freemium subscription** — free tier + Premium (advanced insights, full plans, unlimited tracking, priority support).
2. **Teleconsultation commission** — platform take-rate on expert consults.
3. **B2B / institutional licensing** — per-seat or flat contracts with colleges, employers, clinics, insurers.
4. **Optional later:** premium programs/cohorts, partner lab test bookings (commission), curated marketplace (carefully, evidence-based only).

### Pricing assumptions (India-calibrated)

| Item | Assumption |
|------|-----------|
| Premium subscription | ₹1,199/year (≈ ₹100/month equiv); annual-dominant |
| Net to you after app-store + payment fees (~22%) | ~₹935/payer/year |
| Paid conversion (% of MAU) | 2% conservative / **3% base** / 5% optimistic |
| Teleconsult avg fee | ₹500 |
| Platform take-rate on consults | 20% → ₹100/consult to you |
| Consult usage | ~8% of MAU/year book, ~1.5 consults each |

*Context: global Health & Fitness apps price around USD 9.70/month, but Indian willingness-to-pay is far lower — hence INR pricing near ₹100/month. Freemium download-to-paid conversion runs ~2% median industry-wide; 3% is an achievable target with strong onboarding and a chronic-condition use case.*

## 8. Financial projections (the core question)

### Base case — gross revenue per year (3% conversion, consumer only)

| MAU | Payers | Subscription | Teleconsult rev | **Total / year** | ~ / month |
|----:|-------:|-------------:|----------------:|-----------------:|----------:|
| **1,000** | 30 | ₹36,000 | ₹12,000 | **₹48,000** (~$565) | ~₹4,000 |
| **10,000** | 300 | ₹3,60,000 | ₹1,20,000 | **₹4,80,000** (~$5.6K) | ~₹40,000 |
| **50,000** | 1,500 | ₹18,00,000 | ₹6,00,000 | **₹24,00,000** (~$28K) | ~₹2,00,000 |
| **100,000** | 3,000 | ₹36,00,000 | ₹12,00,000 | **₹48,00,000** (~$56K) | ~₹4,00,000 |

### Sensitivity to conversion (at 100,000 MAU)

| Conversion | Subscription | + Teleconsult | **Total / year** |
|-----------:|-------------:|--------------:|-----------------:|
| 2% (conservative) | ₹24,00,000 | ₹12,00,000 | ₹36,00,000 |
| 3% (base) | ₹36,00,000 | ₹12,00,000 | ₹48,00,000 |
| 5% (optimistic) | ₹60,00,000 | ₹12,00,000 | ₹72,00,000 |

### The explicit "1,000 users" answer

At 1,000 active users, expect roughly **₹40,000–₹70,000 per year gross** (≈ ₹3,500–6,000/month) depending on conversion. After app-store/payment cuts and any server cost, that's effectively **pilot-scale, not income.** The takeaway isn't "this won't work" — it's that **1,000 users is a validation milestone, not a revenue milestone.** Plan your finances around 50K–100K+ engaged users.

### Why B2B changes the early picture

One institutional deal can dwarf small-scale consumer revenue:

| B2B example | Annual value |
|-------------|-------------:|
| 1 college, 5,000 students @ ₹40/student/yr | ₹2,00,000 |
| 1 employer wellness contract | ₹1,00,000–5,00,000 |
| 5 institutional deals (Year 1–2) | ₹10–25 lakh |

So **3–5 B2B contracts can match the consumer revenue of ~50,000 MAU** — which is why institutional sales is the realistic early revenue engine.

## 10. Unit economics

| Metric | Estimate |
|--------|---------:|
| ARPU (paying user, gross) | ~₹1,200/yr |
| Paying-user lifetime (annual plans retain ~44%/yr) | ~1.8 years |
| **LTV (net)** | ~₹1,700 |
| **Target CAC (paying user)** | keep < ₹500–600 |
| Per-install cost (paid ads, India) | ~₹30–120 |

Because LTV is modest, **organic, community, creator, and clinic-referral acquisition must dominate** — paid ads only work once you've proven conversion and retention.

## 11. Key assumptions & risks

**Assumptions to validate early:** activation rate (~35%), retention (chronic condition should beat the harsh industry baselines), 3% conversion, ₹100/month willingness-to-pay, teleconsult uptake.

**Risks:**
- *Retention risk* — health apps churn hard; mitigate with habit loops, community, and genuine clinical value.
- *Trust/regulatory risk* — DPDP Act penalties up to ₹250 crore; telemedicine rules; mishandled data is existential.
- *Monetization risk* — Indian willingness-to-pay is low; B2B may be the real model.
- *Clinical-liability risk* — never "diagnose"; always screen-and-refer with disclaimers.
- *Competition* — incumbents have scale; win on specialization, trust, and localization.
- *Market reality* — only ~5% of health apps reach ~$10K revenue in 2 years.

---

## 12. Funding & path to sustainability

- **Bootstrap option:** lead with B2B/services + a lean consumer app; reach break-even via institutional contracts.
- **Funded option:** raise a small seed (₹50 lakh–₹2 crore) for 18–24 months of runway to chase the 100K-MAU scale where consumer economics start working.
- **Break-even rough target:** ~75K–150K engaged MAU at ≥3% conversion *plus* a handful of B2B deals, depending on burn.
